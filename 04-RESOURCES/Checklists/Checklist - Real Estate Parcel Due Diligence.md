## Overview

This checklist attempts to provide an exhaustive, comprehensive list of the various necessary items to perform an assessment, due diligence, or valuation of a particular real estate parcel.   

This comprehensive evaluation examines a target parcel's investment viability through multiple analytical lenses. The assessment encompasses legal standing, valuation metrics, regulatory compliance, and market positioning to provide actionable intelligence for acquisition decisions.

Analysis of existing systems and methods in the wild reveals critical factors influencing parcel suitability, including current assessed valuations, zoning classifications, and development constraints. Key findings address both immediate acquisition considerations and long-term value creation opportunities, enabling informed strategic decision-making in alignment with investment objectives.

The following sections present detailed findings across nine critical evaluation domains, culminating in a suitability determination and strategic recommendations for proceeding with this investment opportunity.

## Parcel Identification & Legal Description

> [!NOTE]
> *Establishing definitive legal identity is the foundation of any real estate investment analysis. The [[Assessor's Parcel Number (APN)]] serves as the unique identifier linking the property to all official records, tax assessments, and legal instruments. Verification through county assessor databases or similar regional platforms ensures accuracy in parcel boundaries and legal descriptions.*

- [ ] Obtain official parcel identification number or its [[Accessor Parcel Number (APN)]]: APN verification through county assessor records with complete legal description including metes and bounds, lot and block designations, and recorded subdivision references
- [ ] Chain of Title: Comprehensive ownership history review spanning minimum 30-year search period, identifying all transfers, conveyances, and recorded interests affecting the property
- [ ] Encumbrances Review: Detailed examination of recorded easements, covenants, conditions, restrictions (CC&Rs), liens, and any other instruments that may limit use or transfer of the property

- Title search and title insurance commitment
- Zoning verification letter
- Easement and encumbrance review
- Entitlement status and conditions of approval
- Utility availability letters

## Physical / Environmental

- Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA)
- Geotechnical investigation (soil bearing capacity, groundwater)
- ALTA/NSPS survey
- Flood zone determination (FEMA FIRM maps)
- Wetlands delineation
- Topographic survey and grading analysis



## Financial

- Market study / absorption analysis
- Construction cost estimates
- Pro forma financial modeling
- Tax assessment review
- Impact fee verification
